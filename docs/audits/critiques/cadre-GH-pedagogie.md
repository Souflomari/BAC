Items otherwise strong: `CI-65`/`CI-66` (sign tables, no computation) genuinely raise reasoning demand, and `CI-72` (sum over [1,3]) is the recognition rung.

**G4 · exponentielle + logarithme.** `a^x` (`:337-365`) is a model confrontation: definition justified as a *prolongation* (`:345-349`), « pourquoi a>0 » answered rather than decreed (`:351`), predict → compute → numeric break (5,55 vs 12). `x^α`, the n-th root and `log_a` are the commit's weakest teaching: see P5 (states instead of décortiquer; one skipped algebraic step; no worked example for the new tool; the inverted monotonicity unstaged) and P6 (`:489`). **G5 · limites-continuite.** The best-integrated wave. `:336-374` builds the image-of-a-segment on the TVI just proved, gives the monotone shortcut *and* then destroys the reflex with x² on [−1;2] (`:364-374`); `:376-417` reuses the *same* x³+x−1 from `:326` so the dichotomy continues an argument instead of starting one (craft worth keeping). **G6 · complexes.** `nombres-complexes-2:244-290` (linéarisation) is the gold-standard passage of the whole commit: motivation first and *honest* (`:248` — why linearise at all: no direct primitive for cos³x), Euler re-derived rather than quoted (`:250-258`), a predict with an explicit written test instruction (`:264` « Teste les deux membres en x=π/3 **avant de continuer** »), an explicitly headed « Confrontation à la prédiction » (`:282`), a second piège explained by its *origin* rather than memorised (`:284` — the 1/2ⁿ comes from the Euler form), and the loop closed on the motivation at `:286-290`. `nombres-complexes-1:478-518` (Z=z²) is nearly as good: the predict at `:496` is genuine, the « s'arrêter à Z » piège is pre-broken by the degree-4 argument at `:498`, the conjugate shortcut at `:506` is real expert economy, and `:512-516` verifies by substitution. **G7 · probabilités.** The variance section is correct, calm and well-sequenced (after espérance `:459`, before the binomiale `:531`), the second formula is *motivated* by cost rather than asserted (`:499`), and the piège at `:515` is the commit's most useful one — it names a wrong result that is *plausibly close* (0,60 vs 0,6196) and says why that makes it dangerous.
