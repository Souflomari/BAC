# Product Vision

> The canonical statement of what this product is and why it exists.
> Every other document, rule, and agent in this project ultimately
> answers to this one. It is stable — it changes rarely, and only when
> the vision itself shifts, never to accommodate a build constraint.

---

## The North Star

Replace private tutoring with something more effective and far cheaper.

Measured by one thing: whether a struggling Moroccan baccalauréat student,
using the app two focused hours a day, actually becomes a student who excels
at the exam.

The product is a patient, omniscient, infinitely-available private tutor —
one with infinite patience, perfect knowledge of exactly what this student
does and doesn't understand, complete command of the Moroccan bac, and the
ability to explain anything five different ways until one lands. For the
price of a subscription instead of the price of a tutor.

---

## What learning feels like

**Guided-primary, free-secondary.** The app *leads*. It opens on "here's
today's session" and walks the student through it, the way a tutor who has a
plan for you leads a session. Free roam of the whole curriculum is always
available — for the student who joined mid-year and is keeping pace with
school, or who has a test Thursday and needs to work on that instead — but it
is never the default. Guided is the front door; the app makes the guided path
so obviously good that most students stay on it, while never trapping someone
who has a reason to roam.

**Long, flow-protected sessions.** The product is built around the long
session — building, rhythmic, with an arc, designed to be sunk into. Two
focused hours that transform a student is a *flow* outcome, not a snacking
outcome. Shorter pickups are possible but are not what the product is
optimized for. Flow takes fifteen to twenty minutes to enter and is fragile;
the session is actively protected so it can form and hold.

## What a notion is

A single notion, taught to the standard of an excellent tutor, contains all
of the following.

**It opens by making the student care.** Before any definition, the student
meets the *reason* the idea exists — the real-world problem it solves, where
it shows up, why it was worth inventing. Hooked the way a great explainer
opens: with momentum, not a definition. The student should feel "I want to
understand this" before being asked to.

**Décortiquer — the concept taken fully apart.** This is the heart. The idea
is explained from the ground up, in plain language, patiently, one piece at a
time. Every abstraction is grounded — concrete before abstract, analogy and
real-world parallel throughout. The mechanism is made *obvious*, not merely
stated: the *why it is true*, not just the *what*. A hard concept gets as many
segments as it needs, each checked before moving on. The student never gets
lost, because the explanation never skips the step where they would get lost.

**It shows expert reasoning out loud.** Worked examples are not printed
solutions — they are an expert *thinking*, with the reasoning exposed at every
step. Not "we factor here" but "we got 0/0, and 0/0 is the signal that
something cancels, so we look for a common factor." The student learns *how an
expert decides* — the thing that separates someone who can run a procedure
from someone who can solve an unfamiliar problem. This is where reasoning and
problem-solving are built, not just knowledge.

**It walks the student up a real graduated ramp.** From easy,
fully-scaffolded problems, through medium, through hard, to *actual past-bac
questions*, to *fresh variations* on those bac questions so nothing can be
memorized. Each rung increases not only in computational difficulty but in
*reasoning demand* — early rungs build fluency ("can you run the procedure"),
later rungs build recognition ("can you see which procedure applies when the
problem is dressed unfamiliarly"), which is exactly what the bac tests and
exactly where students fail. Scaffolding fades as the student climbs. By the
top, real bac questions feel routine — because the student was deliberately
built up to them, not thrown at them.

**It knows the student.** Every interaction is diagnostic. Wrong answers
reveal *which* misconception is running — not just that something is wrong. The
system knows what this student has mastered, what is slipping, what they are
ready for next, and where the foundational gaps are. It brings earlier
material back right before it would be forgotten. It never advances a student
onto something they are not ready for, and never wastes their time on
something they already have. "What should I do today" always has a real,
personalized answer.

**It is grounded in the real, throughout.** Examples, exercises, and
explanations are drawn from real life wherever possible — ideas connected to
things the student can picture and care about, not abstract symbols floating
in a vacuum.

## What tools it uses

Every teaching modality the concept calls for — text, generated imagery,
generated video, narration, manipulable interactives, simulations, embedded
tools (Desmos, GeoGebra, PhET), diagrams — layered when layering helps, the
way a tutor uses voice, whiteboard, gesture, and story at once.

But every element is summoned by *learning*, never by a need to entertain. A
visual appears because the idea is visual. A video plays because the idea
needs motion. An interactive appears because manipulation is the path to
understanding. Narration speaks because hearing it helps. **The moment a
modality serves stimulation over comprehension, it is cut.** This is the
seductive-details principle applied to the whole experience: richness that
does not serve understanding lowers learning, even when it feels engaging.

## How it feels — flow over stimulation

**Calm, deep, immersive — closer to a focused study environment than a game.**
One thing at a time on screen. Generous space. Calm transitions. No gratuitous
motion, no reward-jingles, no dopamine theater. Richness appears *inside* the
calm when a concept needs it, then recedes. Even the generated assets are
*calm and clarifying* — closer to a quiet, beautifully-illustrated
walk-through than a fast-paced edit — never punchy or attention-grabbing for
its own sake.

This is what distinguishes a tutoring session from an edutainment app. A great
tutor with a student in flow is *calm* — not performing, not juggling
stimulation, not competing with a phone for attention. Deep in one thing, the
student absorbed. That is the feel.

**Design principles that follow:**
- One thing at a time. The student is never choosing between competing stimuli.
- Modality shifts are motivated by the *content*, not the *clock*. The app does
  not cut to a video every ninety seconds to hold attention; it cuts to a video
  when *this* needs a video.
- Calm visual language by default; richness summoned only when the concept
  requires it.
- No engagement theater in the learning core — no confetti, no compulsion
  loops, no notification pressure.
- The long session is protected: minimal interruption, smooth continuity from
  one beat to the next.

## Engagement — aligned, not addictive

There are two distinct problems, and they require opposite solutions:
**getting the student to come back tomorrow** (retention) and **keeping the
student focused during today's session** (flow). The dopamine-engagement
machinery that is good at the first is destructive to the second. Instagram is
the proof: superb at pulling you back, by destroying your capacity to focus.
The product must not rebuild that disease inside the cure.

The resolution: **engagement mechanics live at the edges of the session and
between sessions — and they reward real progress, not mere usage. The learning
core is protected from all of it.**

**Where engagement belongs (and should be strong):**
- *The return.* Streaks, daily goals, a reason to come back tomorrow —
  encountered when the student opens and finishes a session, never during
  learning.
- *Progress made vivid.* The honest dopamine, and the strongest one available
  here: the mastery map filling in, bac-readiness climbing, "you went from 40%
  to 75% on Analyse." Not artificial reward — accurate feedback about genuine
  progress toward the thing the student desperately wants. Be generous here.
- *Earned milestones.* Genuinely hard things — conquering a notion, nailing a
  past-bac question they could not do a month ago — deserve a real moment. Rare
  enough to stay meaningful; not confetti-for-everything, which devalues it.
- *The narrative of getting better.* The most powerful retention mechanic is
  the student *feeling themselves become good* at something they thought they
  were bad at. Made visible and felt, this pull beats any notification.

**Where engagement is forbidden:**
- Inside the learning beat. While understanding a concept or working a problem,
  nothing competes for attention. The reward here is *understanding itself* —
  the click of "I get it," which is genuine and is the right pleasure to
  reinforce.
- Variable/compulsion reward loops in the core. Compulsion is the opposite of
  flow.
- Anything that interrupts the session and pulls the student out of depth.

**The North Star metric is learning-per-hour, never time-on-app.** The app
wants the student to learn efficiently and go live their life. A student who
excels in two focused hours a day and tells their friends is worth more than
one trapped for four hours and taught nothing. Optimizing for time-on-app
would waste a bac student's scarce time to juice a metric — unethical and, with
an exam looming, actively harmful.

## Per subject — because "well-taught" differs

"Well-taught" is a different operation for each subject. This is not stylistic;
it is structural.

**Mathématiques — worked procedure + conceptual visualization.** Mostly
procedural and hierarchical (limits → derivatives → integrals chaining through
well-defined steps), which is worked-example/fading territory. But the
conceptual layer — what a derivative *is*, what an integral *is* — is
load-bearing for real mastery and demands *manipulable* interactives (drag the
point, predict the tangent, watch it update), wired to the symbolic notation
(curve ↔ equation ↔ numeric value moving together), because the bac is written
and symbolic. Self-explanation prompts target *method selection* ("why this
approach, not that one").

**Physique-Chimie — confront the wrong model.** Students arrive with
pre-existing wrong physical models that cannot be displaced by presenting the
correct model cleanly. The misconception must be surfaced and broken
(predict-then-reveal, confront the contradiction). Three modes, all required:
conceptual models, procedural problem-solving, and experimental reasoning
(reading TP data, which the bac tests). This is where interactive simulations
pay off most.

**SVT — worked argument + schema construction.** Not "lots to memorize." Three
things at once: declarative load (where retrieval practice genuinely earns its
place — the one subject where flashcard-like work is partly right, never
sufficient alone), systemic mechanisms (reconstruct the mechanism, not run a
procedure), and argumentation (the SVT épreuve is largely *raisonnement
scientifique* — a worked *argument* shown fully, then faded). SVT thinking is
visual, so it needs real schema-construction interactions (drawing, labeling),
not displayed images. Génétique is the exception — its procedural core gets the
mathématiques profile.

## Built to grow into more, later

These are real future directions. They shape how the foundation is built —
the foundation is designed *toward* them — but they are not part of the core
and are not built now.

**Tutor voice and persona.** Many tutors, many voices — a consistent teacher
presence the student builds rapport with. Deferred. But content is authored
*voice-ready* from the start: written to be spoken aloud, conversational, the
way a person talks — so narration and personas drop in cleanly later instead of
requiring a rewrite. (This is a content rule *now*, even though the persona
itself comes later.)

**Adaptive explanation.** The explanation *itself* morphing per student —
different analogies, different depth, re-explaining a stuck concept a different
way. The eventual AI-at-runtime layer, and the natural home for a live model
re-teaching what a student is stuck on. Deferred, but designed toward: the
foundation personalizes *which* notion and *when* over content authored once,
very well; the harder version layers on later.

**Social and competitive.** Study groups, shared progress, classmate
comparison. A considered future direction — *not* part of the core. If ever
pulled forward, it must be opt-in (the student chooses to join, never imposed)
and bounded toward collaboration and shared-progress framing rather than raw
ranking. The reason it is deferred and bounded: the mission is taking a
*struggling* student to excellent, and that student starts near the bottom —
where a leaderboard demoralizes rather than motivates. The struggling student
is protected from its downside. The individual transformation is a sufficient
motivator on its own; social is optimization on top of an already-sufficient
foundation, and belongs later if at all.

---

## In one sentence

A calm, deep, infinitely-patient tutor that takes any notion fully apart,
grounds it in the real, shows expert reasoning, and walks a struggling student
up to bac mastery — using every teaching modality the concept calls for but
never stimulation for its own sake — so that two focused hours a day replaces
private tutoring with something more effective and far cheaper.
